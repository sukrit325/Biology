Hypotheses & Likelihood Ratio Principle

In forensic paternity casework, the strength of the genetic evidence is formulated as a Likelihood Ratio (LR) comparing two mutually exclusive propositions:

H₁ (Prosecution / Mother): The alleged father is the biological father.
H₂ (Defense / Alternative): An unrelated, random male from the population is the biological father.

$$LR = rac{P( ext{Evidence} \mid H_1)}{P( ext{Evidence} \mid H_2)}$$ Because the 20 CODIS core STR loci reside on separate chromosomes (or exhibit no genetic linkage), the Combined Likelihood Ratio (CLR) is computed as the product of all independent locus Paternity Indices: $$ ext{CLR} = \prod_{i=1}^{20} ext{PI}_i$$

Population Database & Essen-Möller Transformation

Allele frequencies are queried from validated NIST/FBI Caucasian STR population surveys loaded dynamically via allele_frequencies.json.

Prior Probability P(H₁): 0.50 (Neutral Bayesian standard)
Posterior Probability (W): --%
Essen-Möller Equation: $W = rac{ ext{CLR} \cdot P(H_1)}{ ext{CLR} \cdot P(H_1) + (1 - P(H_1))} imes 100\%$
Mutation Handling: Locus-specific mutation rates ($\mu$) derived from NIST/AABB literature. Single-step repeat changes ($\pm 1$ or $\pm 0.2$) are downweighted by $\mu / 2p$ rather than immediately treated as definitive non-paternity.
